How I work

  • 01

    Model the data before the screens

    Tables, roles, and permissions come first. Every interface after that is a view over something that already makes sense — which is why features added in month four don't turn into migrations.

  • 02

    Ship weekly, demo weekly

    You get a link, not a status update. Scope stays fixed and direction stays correctable, so nobody discovers a misunderstanding in week six.

  • 03

    The boring parts decide month three

    Error states, empty states, bad connections, and the person who has to edit this after me. That is the whole distance between a demo and something in production.

About

I started with network cables and ended up writing software.

My first real technical work was network infrastructure — an internship at PUSHANSIBER during my SMK TKJ track, spent tracing why things broke. That habit stuck. I moved into software because I wanted to build the thing, not just keep it running, and the debugging instinct came with me.

Now I'm an Informatics student at Cyber University and I lead its coding community, which mostly means shipping under real deadlines with people who are still learning. I work full-stack, lean heavily on Next.js and Postgres, and still take IoT work when a project needs something physical to happen.

I care about the parts nobody screenshots: data models that don't need rewriting, states that are actually handled, pages that load on a bad connection.
